The New Springfield Regional Justice Center

A new courthouse. A new neighborhood.
A new chapter for Springfield.

Liberty Junction's proposal for the new Springfield Regional Justice Center delivers a safer place to work, the greatest taxpayer value, and lasting growth for the North End and Springfield's economy. The proposal prioritizes site accessibility, inclusive design practices, and the expansion of opportunities for local and minority-owned businesses to meaningfully engage in the project.

The new Springfield Regional Justice Center is finally being built, and it's long overdue.

A $2 billion investment in Springfield to revitalize a neighborhood.

$400M+ Projected taxpayer savings compared with the losing bid, according to arguments presented in Hampden County Superior Court. (Western Mass News, Aug. 11, 2026)

For more than 50 years, thousands of people have worked and attended trials in the old courthouse under conditions nobody should face, and many have become sick or died as a result. The new courthouse has been delayed for too long, and for all the wrong reasons.

Today, Springfield has the right plan, an experienced team, and a bright future for the neighborhood. The courthouse will ensure “Access for All” to every Hampden County resident and will serve as a catalyst for meaningful progress in Springfield for years to come.

The new Springfield Regional Justice Center will be a modern, six-story courthouse at 125 Liberty Street in downtown Springfield. The building will house the Trial Court, Hampden Superior Court, Family and Probate Court, District Attorney's office, Register of Probate, Registry of Deeds, and Clerk of Courts. The design brings daylight throughout the building and into every courtroom, and creates a safe, accessible environment for everyone who works in or visits the facility. The site is within walking distance of public transit at Union Station and ensures access to the facility from all areas of Hampden County.

The new courthouse will bring jobs and activity to the neighborhood, creating a center of economic development that will help revitalize this part of Springfield. Building the Springfield Regional Justice Center will create approximately 2,000–2,500 construction jobs and generate additional opportunities for community participation.

When the Springfield Regional Justice Center is complete, it will serve as a hub for civic services and strengthen the neighborhood. The activity this project generates will attract more people to the surrounding businesses and create opportunities for long-term economic growth in the area.

The deal

A competitively reviewed deal that saves taxpayers hundreds of millions.

DCAMM selected Liberty Junction's proposal for the Hampden County Courthouse after evaluating multiple bidders. State officials, and now a Hampden County judge, have found the deal delivers real value, and found no merit in the losing bidders' legal challenge.

What is at stake

Hampden County's Trial Court employees, litigants, and the public are currently served by an aging courthouse. The new Hampden County Courthouse at 125 Liberty Street is intended to replace it with a modern, safe, accessible facility. Every additional month of litigation is a month those needs go unmet.

Liberty Junction's proposal was selected through DCAMM's procurement process. The company includes CoJo Partners and other development partners.

"There is no legal or logical support for the plaintiffs' convoluted theory that DCAMM somehow violated [the conflict-of-interest law] in the procurement process." Hampden County Superior Court Justice David Hodge, August 24, 2026 ruling.

What the review found

Arguments presented in Hampden County Superior Court put the value of the selected proposal at $400 million or more in taxpayer savings compared with the losing bid. Justice Hodge's ruling separately found that Liberty Junction's total estimated cost of occupancy is $7.4 million lower than the next-closest finalist, and that the deal saves the Commonwealth roughly $600,000 per month compared with other proposals.

What the court decided

On August 24, 2026, Justice Hodge denied Balise and Patel's motions for a temporary restraining order and a preliminary injunction in a 29-page ruling. He found the plaintiffs had not shown a likelihood of success on the merits of their conflict-of-interest claim, and that DCAMM, not being a state employee, is not subject to the conflict-of-interest law the plaintiffs cited. The ruling did not dismiss the underlying case, and Balise and Patel have since filed a notice of appeal. The Massachusetts Attorney General has moved to dismiss the case entirely, calling the plaintiffs' legal theories "incorrect and inapplicable."

Who is challenging the deal, and why

The lawsuit was filed on July 9, 2026 by USPB JV, LLC, owned by New England used-car dealer Jeb Balise, and Springfield Tower Square LLC, owned by real-estate investor Dinesh Patel. Both entities submitted losing bids for the same courthouse contract that Liberty Junction won. Liberty Junction has described their suit as an attempt "to delay the project and recoup a lost bid." Co-founder Richard Mann said at the August 11 hearing: "Jeb Balise intends to stall that process."

Every delay has a cost

Liberty Junction has urged "the competitors to think of Hampden County residents and courthouse workers" and stop what it called "baseless legal delay tactics." A pending appeal and the Attorney General's motion to dismiss mean the case is not yet fully resolved. But the trial court's initial review has already tested the plaintiffs' central claims and found them unsupported.

Key facts

  • $400M+ in projected taxpayer savings (court arguments, Aug. 11)
  • $7.4M lower total occupancy cost than the next-closest finalist (Judge Hodge, Aug. 24)
  • ~$600,000/month savings vs. other proposals (Judge Hodge, Aug. 24)
  • TRO and injunction denied, Aug. 24, 2026

The real choice

Every month of delay has a cost.

This isn't a choice between "fast" and "careful." A judge has already reviewed the process and found the challengers' core legal theory has no support. Continued litigation from the losing bidders doesn't add scrutiny the deal hasn't already survived. It risks the savings and schedule Hampden County was promised.

The record is public: a competitive review, a signed evaluation, and a court ruling that already tested the objections raised against it and rejected them.

And there's a human cost.

The current Hampden County courthouse (the Roderick Ireland Courthouse) has a documented history of serious employee illness.

2 judges

Two Springfield District Court judges who worked out of the same judicial chambers, Robert F. Kumor Jr. (died 2013) and William J. Boyle (died 2019), both died of ALS, and a longtime employee whose office sat one floor above them died of ALS in 2017.

Boston Globe
5 ALS deaths

Mayor Domenic Sarno has stated publicly that five people who worked in the building have died of ALS and that 60 cancer cases have been linked to courthouse workers.

City of Springfield
25+ claims

A 2021 class-action lawsuit alleged at least 25 former long-term employees had died of cancer; the building was later evaluated by NIOSH and the case was settled in 2022.

Boston Globe
Toxic mold

Independent testing conducted for a related lawsuit found toxic mold in the building's vents, walls, and carpeting.

WAMC

An environmental consultant's testing did not identify a specific cause, and medical causation has not been established, but a Harvard environmental epidemiologist called the concentration of ALS cases in one building "suggestive of some environmental exposure."

Every month spent in litigation over the replacement is another month Trial Court employees keep reporting to that building.

About this campaign

For a courthouse Hampden County doesn't have to keep waiting for.

Finish the Hampden County Courthouse is a public-information campaign supporting the Liberty Junction proposal for a new Hampden County courthouse and opposing further delay of a competitively reviewed, court-tested deal.

Our position

Hampden County's Trial Court employees, litigants, and residents need a modern courthouse, and they need it without unnecessary delay. DCAMM ran a competitive procurement, selected a proposal that state officials say saves taxpayers real money, and a Hampden County judge has already reviewed and rejected the losing bidders' central legal claims.

We believe continued litigation aimed at re-fighting a decided procurement doesn't protect the public. It risks the savings, the schedule, and the modern courthouse Hampden County has been promised.

Our standards

  • Rely on court rulings and published reporting, with direct links to the source.
  • Attribute characterizations of motive to the party that made them. We do not allege wrongdoing beyond what the record shows.
  • Describe the case accurately, including that an appeal and a motion to dismiss remain pending.
  • Correct material errors promptly and visibly as the case develops.
  • Disclose relevant facts even when they complicate the story, including Liberty Junction's own ties to Barros and Harris.

Who should care

Trial Court employees, court users, downtown Springfield residents and businesses, taxpayers across Hampden County, and anyone who wants a needed public project to move forward on the timeline and budget the public was promised.
